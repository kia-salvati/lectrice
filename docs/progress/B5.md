# B5: read-ahead and the bounded audio cache (done 2026-10-23)

## What was built
- **Planner** (`lectrice-app/src/speech/read_ahead_plan.rs`, pure): `plan_read_ahead(ReadAheadInput { document, from, rate,
  read_skipped, minutes })` → the sentences after `from`, in playback order, until `minutes × 60 × rate` seconds of 1x audio
  (18 chars/s of `spoken_text()`; the last sentence may overshoot). `playback_order` matches the frontend's
  `buildPlaybackOrder` (`frontend/src/utils/playbackOrder.ts`): a skipped block plays its cue when `readSkipped` is off
  (captions have none) and its own sentences, never the cue, when it's on. A `from` that isn't playable starts at the first
  playable sentence of its block or later (like `resolvePlayable`). Sentences with empty speech text are left out (they are
  0.25 s of silence and never cached).
- **`ReadAhead`** (`speech/read_ahead.rs`), called by `SpeakSentence` on every audio request (cache hit, miss or silence):
  1. `retain_around(key, keep_minutes_per_document × 60 × rate)` on the cache.
  2. One plan per (document, voice). The same position + rate + readSkipped (+ cleaner version, engine) → nothing to do.
     Otherwise it re-plans. Old jobs the new plan still wants are kept, the others are **cancelled**. New sentences are
     skipped if `contains` says they are cached, else submitted with `submit_with` at `ReadAhead` priority. The callback
     (worker thread) encodes + `put`s and removes its own entry (a token guards against a newer job for the same sentence).
     At most 4 plans are kept (least recently used dropped, its jobs cancelled).
  3. On a miss the `Now` job is submitted **before** the read-ahead, so the requested sentence goes first.
  Errors in read-ahead or eviction are `warn`ed and never fail playback.
- **Eviction** (`lectrice-infra/src/storage/audio_cache.rs`): `FsAudioCache::open(data_dir, max_bytes)` scans the cache once
  (temp files and foreign folders ignored) into an in-memory index `{ scope → BTreeMap<sentence, {size, written}> }` plus a
  total. Scope = document + cleaner version + engine + voice. `put` and evictions keep it up to date. Durations come from
  the file size (`(size − 44) / 48 000` per second). `retain_around`: inside the listener's scope, delete the farthest
  behind first, then the farthest ahead, until ≤ keep seconds; the clip at the position is never deleted. Then, if the
  total exceeds `cache_max_mb`, delete the oldest clips of **other** scopes. Clips being read (`get`) or written (`put`) are
  marked in use under the same lock and are skipped. Deletes run under that lock, so no reader can start on a file that's
  being deleted.
- **New domain port** `lectrice-domain/src/audio_retention.rs`: `AudioRetention { contains, retain_around }` and
  `AudioEviction` (counts only), plus two lines in domain `lib.rs`. `FsAudioCache` implements it; `Adapters.audio_retention`
  is the same object as `audio_cache`.
- **Document cache** (`lectrice-infra/src/storage/cached_library.rs`): `CachedLibrary<L>` wraps the repository and keeps
  the 4 most recently found parsed documents (`Arc<Document>`, LRU). `save` drops the entry (before and after the write; a
  save counter stops a read that raced with a save from being cached). Wired in `composition::build_state`.
- **Logs** (no text): `read-ahead planned { docId, voiceId, sentenceId, rate, readSkipped, planned, plannedSeconds, queued,
  kept, cached, cancelled }`, `read-ahead audio cached { docId, sentenceId, voiceId, bytes }`, `audio cache evicted
  { keepSeconds, windowClips, capClips, freedBytes, keptSeconds, cacheBytes }` (only when something was deleted),
  `Audio cache opened { cacheMaxMb, scanMs }`.

## Manual check (debug build, scratch data dir, port 7980, 8 threads, DDS PDF)
- Import: 2402 sentences. `GET …/sentences/0/audio?voice=af_heart&rate=1` → 200 in 0.28 s.
  Plan: **110 sentences, 600 s estimated**. Clips on disk (incl. sentence 0) at 10 s steps:
  10, 18, 26, 35, 43, **50 at 60 s (282 s of audio)**, then 69/76/82/93/101/107 and **all 111 after ~130 s, 635 s of real
  audio** (estimate within 6 %). That is ≈ 4.9× real time, so 10 min ahead is ready ~2 min after starting.
- `sentence 1, rate=2`: planned 192 (1211 s ≈ 2 × 600), cached 109 skipped, queued 83.
  5 s later a far jump to `sentence 1500, rate=2`: **cancelled 81** (2 had finished), queued 226. The jump request took
  1.83 s (it waited for the running read-ahead sentence, which can't be stopped, then its own synthesis). Over the run the
  worker synthesized 131 clips and 130 were cached: the running job cancelled by the jump was synthesized and dropped.
- Restart with `read_ahead_minutes = keep_minutes = 1` (scan took 1 ms for 130 clips), request sentence 1510 at rate 1:
  **evicted 116 clips (31.8 MB), farthest behind first**; 57.8 s kept (sentences 1503–1518 left). No warnings or errors in
  either log; SIGTERM shut down cleanly.

## Deviations
1. **New port instead of changing `AudioCache`** (`ports.rs` is frozen): `AudioRetention` sits in its own domain file.
2. `FsAudioCache::new(dir)` became `open(dir, max_bytes) -> AppResult` (it scans). `SpeakSentence::new` takes a
   `SpeakSentenceDeps` struct (6 dependencies). `Settings` has `read_ahead: ReadAheadSettings`.
3. **The global cap is applied on each audio request** (in `retain_around`), not on every `put`. Between two requests the
   cache can go over the cap by at most one read-ahead (≈ 30–60 MB at 10 min). The listener's own scope is never cut by the
   cap (it's already bounded by its window); if one window is bigger than the cap, the cap is exceeded.
4. The window is measured in **sentence-id order** with the real clip lengths, not in playback order: ids follow the
   document, so "behind" and "ahead" are the same either way.
5. Cancelling a job that is already running drops its audio (the worker API can't keep it), as seen in the manual check.
   Empty voice/document folders are left on disk after eviction.
6. The frontend prefetches the next 2 sentences at `Now` priority, so the anchor moves a sentence or two ahead and back.
   That costs at most one tail job cancelled and re-queued per step (seen in tests as `kept 59, queued 1`).

## Notes for B6
- Export jobs should reuse cached clips via `AudioCache::get` and submit misses at `SpeechPriority::Export` with `submit`
  on their own thread (not from a callback). `playback_order(document, read_skipped)` gives the order to export.
- Export misses are not put in the cache by default; if B6 caches them, they count toward the global cap but not toward the
  listener's window (other scope only if the voice differs), and the next request may evict them behind the position.
- Every audio request moves the window; exporting while listening is fine because eviction skips in-use clips.
